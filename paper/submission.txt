Title
EggFuse: Exploiting Locality in E-Graphs

Authors
Minakami Yuki

Suggested primary category
cs.PL (Programming Languages)

Abstract
Equality-saturation engines share equivalent terms, but repeatedly execute related queries, construct intermediate bindings and maintain connections between rule applications. EggFuse exploits structural, temporal and storage locality through Cell templates and their actual instances. The compiler describes query completion, ordered effects and cross-stage Entry projections with a common slot layout. The runtime reuses actual result witnesses while retaining residual matching, equality repair and source-schedule visibility. Optional workers group query continuations by Cell; ordered graph effects remain coordinated. Offline iterative Bake uses observed applications to propose larger reusable structures and checks which queries, handoffs or effects they can discharge. It is one optimization mechanism within this architecture. A new audit of 127 unmodified upstream egglog inputs yields 26 exact final-state passes; 95 inputs are rejected by capability checks, five fail full compilation and one times out. On 288 captured eggcc pass inputs, single-thread inference totals 140.335 seconds for the version-2.0 native baseline and 53.789 seconds for Cell, a ratio of 2.609, with 727.05 seconds of offline preparation. A separate paired native-version comparison gives a 1.440 aggregate inference ratio for the 2.0 baseline over the 3.0 release. These are execution comparisons, not suite-wide certified compiler speedups. Separately pinned learning and storage studies show why fewer lookups or smaller snapshots can still increase runtime maintenance. The evidence motivates sharing execution state and removing redundant maintenance together.
